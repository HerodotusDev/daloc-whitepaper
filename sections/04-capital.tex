\section{Capital Management}

The origination pipelines (Section~\ref{sec:lifecycle}) differ fundamentally in how much capital the vault must commit.

\subsection{Vault-Funded Path}

The vault commits principal at 1:1. No variable-rate leg, no hedging buffers. Yield per loan is higher but capital leverage is lower.

\subsection{Externally-Sourced Path}

The vault commits only 3--7\% of principal as hedging reserves (rate buffer plus option buffer), achieving 15--30$\times$ capital leverage. Pledged collateral simultaneously secures the \daloc{} loan and the external venue position. External venues are suppliers, not competitors: the platform aggregates their liquidity rather than competing for deposits.

\subsection{Capital Efficiency}

The risk engine sizes both buffers at quote time: the rate buffer from tail simulations of variable-rate paths, the option buffer from simulated round-trip swap execution against on-chain pool depth.

Consider two scenarios for a \$500,000 loan backed by WETH collateral at 70\% LTV:

\textbf{Vault-funded.} The vault transfers \$350,000 USDC directly to the borrower. Capital committed: \$350,000 at 1:1. The vault earns the full 8\% fixed rate on that principal, approximately \$6,900 over a 90-day term.

\textbf{Externally-sourced.} The external venue supplies the \$350,000 principal. The vault reserves \$17,500 as a rate buffer (5\% of principal), approximately \textbf{20:1 capital leverage}. The vault earns the buffer surplus: the difference between the reserved \$17,500 and the realized variable-rate cost, typically \$3,000--\$8,000 over a 90-day term.

At close, buffer surplus remains in the vault as LP yield; buffer shortfall reduces vault NAV.
