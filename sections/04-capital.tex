\section{Capital Management}
\label{sec:capital}

The underwriter is the canonical capital source. When available LP capital exceeds the requested principal, a creation proof executes a direct-funded transfer from the underwriter's allowance to the borrower; the underwriter receives debt tokens as the creditor of record. The operator produces the proof but never custodies tokens. When demand exceeds available capital, the creation proof's hook chain sources principal from external lending venues instead, reserving underwriter capital only for hedging instruments that cover funding mismatch (rate buffers for fixed-rate loans; index-linked reserves for variable-rate loans, per underwriter policy). The underwriter's committed policy (Section~\ref{sec:lp}) governs the allocation between paths.

\subsection{Direct-Funded Path}

The underwriter deploys principal directly to the borrower (or to the margin agent for Prime Trade). The CDP holds collateral as a liquidation hedge; no external venue is involved. Capital is committed at 1:1 with no variable-rate mismatch or hedging buffers, and the underwriter captures the full coupon (fixed or variable, depending on the rate model). This path is preferred whenever available capital permits.

\subsection{Liquidity-Forwarding Path}

When available LP capital is insufficient (because existing deployments have consumed it, or because the loan size exceeds available balance), the creation proof's hook chain sources principal from established lending venues via the CDP rather than from the underwriter's balance sheet directly; the operator produces the proof but never custodies tokens. The CDP deposits pledged collateral, or an approved intermediate asset after a collateral swap, into an admissible external venue, borrows the market's supported principal asset against it, and routes the borrowed asset to the borrower or margin agent. For the initial USDC-based markets, admissible venues may include Aave~\cite{aave2026}, Morpho~\cite{morphoblue2023}, and Spark~\cite{spark2023}. The underwriter commits only a fraction of principal as hedging reserves (rate buffer plus collateral conversion reserve), achieving high capital leverage. Pledged collateral simultaneously secures the \daloc{} loan and the external venue position.

External venues are not selected arbitrarily by operators at execution time. Each market defines its admissible venue set through its zero-knowledge programs and authorized hook adapters. A venue is admissible only if its collateral support, principal asset, oracle behavior, liquidation mechanics, rate model, and on-chain state can be verified by the market's programs and incorporated into the risk engine. Changing the venue set, adapter, or verification logic therefore defines a different market.

When underwriter capital is used for hedging reserves, the underwriter's committed policy may impose stricter constraints than the market itself: allowed venues, maximum exposure per venue, maximum utilization or borrow-rate assumptions, minimum liquidity depth, liquidation-latency bounds, and adapter-level risk limits. The creation proof attests that liquidity-forwarding activity stays within this policy; a missing or invalid proof prevents origination.

\subsection{Capital Efficiency}

Both buffers on the liquidity-forwarding path are sized at quote time by the provable risk engine (Section~\ref{sec:risk}): the rate buffer from tail simulations of variable-rate paths, the collateral conversion reserve from simulated round-trip execution costs. For a liquidity-forwarding loan, the underwriter's capital leverage is
\begin{equation}
  \kappa = \frac{P}{B_{\mathrm{rate}} + B_{\mathrm{conv}}},
  \label{eq:leverage}
\end{equation}
where $P$ is the loan principal and $B_{\mathrm{rate}} + B_{\mathrm{conv}}$ is the total hedging capital committed. On the direct-funded path the underwriter commits the full principal ($\kappa = 1$ by definition); on the liquidity-forwarding path, typical combined buffers of ${\sim}$5\% of principal achieve $\kappa \approx 20$. The blend between paths depends on available LP capital and market demand.

At close, buffer surplus remains as LP yield; buffer shortfall reduces underwriter NAV.
