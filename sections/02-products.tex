\section{Products}

\subsection{Borrow}

Borrow lets a user lock collateral on one chain and receive a fixed-rate, fixed-term loan denominated in a principal asset on another. The rate, fee, and total repayment are quoted upfront and locked for the life of the loan---the borrower faces no variable-rate exposure. The pledged collateral stays on its home chain and continues to accrue whatever native yield it would normally earn; no bridging or unstaking is required. The borrower receives the principal directly and uses it however they want. Origination is handled through the platform's unified order book: the borrower's request enters the book as a standard entry, and solvers respond with quotes that are structurally identical objects on the other side---the matching engine treats both symmetrically.

\subsection{Prime Trade}

Prime Trade is \daloc{}'s prime brokerage product on Hyperliquid---and the platform's most distinctive offering. It is a credit origination product for holders of yield-bearing assets on Ethereum, Arbitrum, or Base who want directional perpetual exposure on Hyperliquid without giving up the underlying.

A holder of staked ETH on Arbitrum wants to trade Hyperliquid perpetuals. The conventional path: unstake, bridge to Hyperliquid, deposit, trade---giving up yield and taking on bridging risk. Prime Trade keeps the original asset where it is. The borrower pledges it as collateral; the platform opens a fixed-rate credit line; the credit is routed to the trading venue where the borrower executes strategies directly. The pledged asset keeps earning its native yield. The entire origination---collateral deposit, external venue supply, USDC borrow, cross-chain bridge---executes as a single atomic hook chain, with no partial state if any step fails.

The borrower never holds the borrowed capital. This is not a credit protocol where capital is handed to a borrower; it is a lending protocol where the loan is deployed into a constrained execution environment. The platform provides credit origination and collateral management---not trade execution. The borrower selects positions through the platform's interface; the per-position margin agent executes those trades while restricting all other capital movements---the borrowed funds cannot be withdrawn, transferred to spot, or lent.

The product fits traders running strategies that benefit from capital efficiency---basis desks, delta-neutral funds, funding arbitrageurs---where the underlying collateral should remain productive. \daloc{} does not take directional exposure to trader PnL---all gains and losses accrue inside the borrower's position. The platform earns from lending spread and builder fees, not from trading outcomes.

In the current release, every credit line is fully backed by pledged collateral. The custody design---protocol-controlled per-position wallets with deterministic addressing---is what will let future releases safely extend under-collateralized leverage. Repayment is mechanical, not discretionary; this is the structural property that makes higher leverage possible.

\subsection{Earn}

Earn lets liquidity providers supply capital into the platform's vault. That capital underwrites the hedging instruments attached to each loan---rate buffers, option buffers, and liquidation reserves---rather than funding loan principal directly. Returns to LPs are blended across whichever strategies are active, net of fees retained by the platform. The vault operator manages capital allocation; the capital itself sits in the vault contract, which---unlike the core protocol contracts---is non-upgradeable, giving LPs a permanent guarantee that the share accounting, withdrawal flow, and redemption fee logic cannot be altered after deployment. Withdrawal is epoch-based with a settlement delay (Section~\ref{subsec:withdrawal}).
