\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Risks}

External liquidity sourcing expands capital efficiency but introduces exposure to the lending venues it relies on. A governance change at the underlying venue --- a reduction in LTV ceilings, an increase in liquidation bonuses, or a modification to rate curves --- can directly affect the economics of externally-sourced positions.

Rate risk and price risk are correlated: in a market crash, collateral value drops while utilization on external venues spikes, raising the variable rate precisely when the collateral buffer shrinks. Joint crash dynamics remain a structural challenge for any fixed-rate lending platform.

At scale, the platform's own borrowing raises utilization on external venues, compressing spreads --- a natural scale ceiling that diversification and direct vault funding mitigate but do not eliminate.

Markets that permit rehypothecation introduce additional risk: the policy may expose positions to correlated failures across venues. Participants must evaluate a market's programs before interacting; the protocol ensures correct execution of the rules, not their prudence.

\subsection{Future Capabilities}

Credit lines are currently bounded by pledged collateral value, and each position is fully isolated. The architecture accommodates a wider range of positions and risk-engine capabilities.

\textbf{Operator ecosystem.} Multiple independent operators can each run their own vault with a distinct mandate. Competition for LP capital creates pressure toward better risk-adjusted returns; the mandate makes comparison meaningful because strategies are constrained to declared bounds.

\textbf{Under-collateralized credit.} The protocol-controlled custody model (Section~\ref{sec:crosschain}), where borrowed capital cannot leave the constrained execution environment, can extend to credit lines exceeding collateral value: the credit decision reduces to risk-engine parameterization rather than trust.

\textbf{Cross-margining.} The cross-margining layer (Section~\ref{sec:positions}) can encode netting rules, barrier adjustments across portfolios, and multi-collateral offset strategies through the market's ZK programs, without modifying core contracts or weakening per-loan isolation.
