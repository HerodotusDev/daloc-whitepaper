\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Risks}

\textbf{External venue dependency.} Governance changes at an underlying venue---reduced LTV ceilings, modified rate curves---directly affect externally-sourced positions. \emph{Mitigation:} vault-funded path requires no external venue; mandate concentration caps limit per-venue exposure.

\textbf{Correlated rate and price risk.} In a crash, collateral value drops while venue utilization spikes, raising the variable rate precisely when the buffer shrinks. \emph{Mitigation:} rate buffers are sized from tail simulations under the joint distribution (Section~\ref{sec:risk}).

\textbf{Rehypothecation risk.} Markets that permit rehypothecation expose positions to correlated venue failures. The protocol enforces correct execution of the rules, not their prudence.

\textbf{Smart contract and circuit risk.} A hub bug could affect all capital flows; a soundness flaw in a settlement program could permit invalid state transitions. \emph{Mitigation:} independent audits, formal verification of accounting invariants, and on-chain invariant monitoring (Section~\ref{sec:settlement}).

\textbf{Oracle risk.} Oracle logic is embedded in each market's ZK programs. A manipulable oracle can produce incorrect liquidation or accrual facts. \emph{Mitigation:} freshness is verified inside the proof; participants choose which markets to interact with.

\subsection{Future Capabilities}

\textbf{Operator ecosystem.} Multiple independent operators each run their own vault with a distinct mandate. Competition for LP capital creates pressure toward better risk-adjusted returns; the mandate makes comparison meaningful because strategies are verified per-epoch against declared bounds.

\textbf{Under-collateralized credit.} Protocol-controlled custody (Section~\ref{sec:crosschain}), where borrowed capital cannot leave the constrained execution environment, extends naturally to credit lines exceeding collateral value---the credit decision reduces to risk-engine parameterization.

\textbf{Cross-margining.} ZK programs can encode netting rules across a borrower's active positions, recognizing offsetting exposure for reduced buffer sizing without weakening per-loan isolation (Section~\ref{sec:positions}).

\section{Conclusion}

\daloc{} unifies cross-chain prime brokerage, fixed-rate lending, and passive LP yield into a single platform enforced by zero-knowledge proofs. The design separates trust-minimized surfaces (market logic, settlement, position isolation) from administered ones (vault operation, risk pricing, solver competition), constraining the latter with cryptographic verification. Capital efficiency, institutional compliance, and cross-chain reach coexist without compromising per-position isolation.
