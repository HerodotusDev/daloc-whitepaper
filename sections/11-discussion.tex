\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Risks}

External liquidity sourcing expands the platform's capital efficiency but introduces exposure to the lending venues it relies on. A governance change at the underlying venue---a reduction in loan-to-value ceilings, an increase in liquidation bonuses, or a modification to rate curves---can directly affect the economics of positions that source liquidity through it.

Rate risk and price risk are not independent. In a market crash, collateral value drops and utilization on external venues spikes simultaneously. The variable rate the platform pays rises precisely when the collateral buffer shrinks. Hedging reserves sized under an assumption of independent rate and price dynamics may prove insufficient in a correlated regime. The risk engine continuously recalibrates against live on-chain data, but joint crash dynamics---where rates spike precisely as prices fall---remain a structural challenge for any fixed-rate lending platform. Vault-funded loans avoid external-venue rate risk but bear full credit risk on the principal deployed---a concentrated default event on vault-funded positions impacts LP NAV directly rather than through buffer exhaustion.

At scale, the platform's own activity can influence the external venues it relies on. A large volume of \daloc{}-originated borrowing increases utilization on the underlying venue, raising its variable rate and reducing the spread between the fixed rate charged to borrowers and the cost of capital. This creates a natural scale ceiling on the externally-sourced path---the platform becomes less competitive as its own footprint grows on any single venue. Diversification across venues and direct vault funding mitigate but do not eliminate the constraint.

Markets that permit rehypothecation introduce additional risk dimensions. While zero-knowledge programs enforce the defined collateral policy cryptographically, the policy itself may expose positions to correlated failures across the venues where collateral is deployed. Participants must evaluate the market's programs before interacting---the protocol ensures correct execution of the rules, not the prudence of the rules themselves.

\subsection{Future Capabilities}

In the current release, credit lines are bounded by pledged collateral value and each position is fully isolated. The architecture is designed to accommodate a wider range of positions and risk-engine capabilities over time.

\textbf{Operator ecosystem.} In the initial release, the platform itself operates the Harvest Vault. The architecture supports multiple independent operators, each running their own vault instance with a distinct mandate---one conservative (high-grade collateral, short tenors, tight concentration limits), another aggressive (broader collateral set, higher LTV ceilings, longer durations). Competition between operators for LP capital creates pressure toward better risk-adjusted returns. LPs choose which vault to deposit into based on the published mandate, historical performance, and fee structure---the mandate makes comparison meaningful because strategies are constrained to declared bounds rather than opaque discretion.

\textbf{Institutional access.} Criteria-based matching is the mechanism that enables regulated counterparties to participate in the unified order book without requiring a permissioned fork. A regulated lender can post quotes restricted to KYC-verified borrowers; the matching engine enforces the restriction without fragmenting the liquidity surface. This path requires no protocol modification---only attestation issuers and criteria definitions. Debt tokens originating from restricted matches carry transfer-time criteria that persist through secondary trading, ensuring compliance survives the instrument's full lifecycle.

\textbf{Refinancing and maturity management.} Solver-driven refinancing reduces the protocol's exposure to cliff events at loan maturity. Instead of forced liquidation when a borrower cannot repay at term, the loan can be rolled into a new position at current market rates---retaining platform AUM, preserving the borrower's collateral position, and avoiding fire-sale liquidation in stressed markets. As the solver network matures, automated refinancing strategies---triggered by rate conditions or approaching maturity---can operate without borrower intervention.

The protocol-controlled custody model described in Section~\ref{sec:crosschain}---where borrowed capital cannot leave the constrained execution environment---can extend to credit lines exceeding collateral value: because the protocol controls the capital and repayment is mechanical, the credit decision reduces to risk-engine parameterization rather than a trust decision.

Cross-margining across positions---aggregating collateral value across a borrower's active loans---can recover the capital efficiency that per-loan isolation foregoes, without abandoning per-loan risk boundaries. Individual CDPs retain their own collateral and liquidation logic, while a higher-level accounting layer recognizes offsetting positions across loans for the purpose of reduced buffer sizing or increased borrowing capacity.

The solver set can expand to additional venues and execution strategies, and the market-definition system can accommodate new collateral types, settlement programs, and collateral policies as they are created and adopted. Because markets, programs, and solvers are independently extensible, the range of supported positions grows without requiring changes to the core protocol contracts. The hook composition system imposes no limit on pipeline complexity---new hook types for new venue interactions or collateral strategies can be added without modifying existing hooks or the settlement layer.
