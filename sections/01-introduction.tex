\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

A holder of yield-bearing collateral on one chain who wants leveraged exposure on another must unstake, bridge, and re-deposit, giving up yield and taking on counterparty risk at each step. On-chain lending exceeds \$28B~\cite{defillama}, yet the dominant pool-based designs serve only variable-rate, open-term use cases on a single chain.

Existing protocols address important fragments of this problem. Aave provides large-scale pooled lending with governance-managed risk parameters, isolation modes, e-mode, and a hub-and-spoke architecture in Aave V4~\cite{aave2026}. Euler V2 provides a modular lending framework through isolated vaults and the Ethereum Vault Connector, letting builders compose customized credit markets~\cite{eulerv2}. Morpho Blue separates lending infrastructure from risk curation through isolated markets, while Morpho's fixed-maturity work moves closer to intent-based, fixed-term credit backed by tokenized debt~\cite{morphoblue2023,midnight2025}. Spark focuses on conservative stablecoin lending and protocol-directed capital allocation through SparkLend and related liquidity infrastructure~\cite{spark2023}. Vault-based systems further improve allocation by curating exposure across markets~\cite{zbandut2025}.

These systems improve capital efficiency, market customization, and risk curation. None simultaneously coordinates structured, term-bounded credit (fixed and variable rate models) across chains, keeps collateral on its home chain while proving its state without trusted relayers, sources capital from external lending venues, converts variable external-venue funding into borrower-facing fixed or index-linked terms, routes borrowed capital into constrained execution environments, and accommodates institutional criteria without fragmenting liquidity. Public materials and roadmaps for the major lending protocols show overlap with individual components, especially modular markets, curated vaults, fixed-term loans, and cross-chain architecture, but not this full combination inside one settlement and verification layer~\cite{aave2026,eulerv2,morphoblue2023,midnight2025,spark2023}.

\daloc{} unifies these capabilities in a single credit coordination and settlement layer. Fixed- and variable-rate term loans, leveraged perpetual trading, and passive yield share a common matching and settlement infrastructure; borrowers, traders, and liquidity providers interact through the same intent structure without bridging collateral or fragmenting liquidity. Capital deploys from underwriter balances when available, or via liquidity forwarding through established lending venues; criteria-based matching accommodates regulated participants alongside open ones. Programmable zero-knowledge markets, a provable risk engine, and permissionless proof-gated execution complete the architecture; independently operated markets isolate liveness and trust assumptions. The decentralization properties of this architecture, including correctness, completeness, and exit guarantees, are analyzed in the companion paper~\cite{daloc-decent2026}. The architecture carries inherent risks (smart-contract vulnerabilities, oracle dependencies, bridge liveness, and proof-system availability) discussed in Sections~\ref{sec:governance} and~\ref{sec:discussion}.
