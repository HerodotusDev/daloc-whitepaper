\section{Governance and Trust Assumptions}

The protocol's administered surface is deliberately narrow. Markets are immutable, vault contracts are non-upgradeable, and every epoch advance requires a valid fact in the facts registry (Section~\ref{sec:settlement}). The two surfaces that require trust are the hub contract and the liveness of off-chain infrastructure.

\subsection{Protocol Administration}

The hub contract is upgradeable, governed by a multisig with timelock. Upgradeability permits response to critical vulnerabilities in the token rail, at the cost of trust in the multisig. Because the hub is the single authorization point for all capital flows (Section~\ref{sec:architecture}), an upgrade can alter routing and pause behavior across every market simultaneously. Pause, unpause, and bridge adapter whitelisting (Section~\ref{sec:crosschain}) are also multisig actions; new cross-chain routes are therefore permissioned even though markets themselves are permissionless.

\subsection{Liveness Dependencies}

The settlement engine's correctness is guaranteed by zero-knowledge proofs (Section~\ref{sec:settlement}), but its liveness is not: if the engine halts, no new epochs advance. Existing positions remain safe --- collateral stays locked, debt is unchanged, intent cancellation remains available --- but undercollateralized positions go undetected until epochs resume.

If no epoch advances within one hour, the protocol enters liveness-failure mode: funds are released for withdrawal and positions may unwind without further settlement proofs.

The risk engine produces LTV limits, rates, and buffer sizes off-chain; the mandate (Section~\ref{sec:lp}) enforces hard floors --- rate floors, LTV ceilings, concentration caps --- but does not validate the model between those bounds.

Cross-chain USDC movement depends on Circle's CCTP attester infrastructure (Section~\ref{sec:crosschain}); attester liveness and censorship properties are inherited, not controlled by the protocol. Cross-chain authorization correctness is cryptographic --- HDP produces Merkle proofs over actual chain state --- but proof-generation availability depends on the provider.
