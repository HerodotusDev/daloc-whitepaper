\section{Loan Lifecycle}
\label{sec:lifecycle}

\subsection{Origination}

A borrower posts a request, the operator matches it against underwriter terms, and the accepted match is committed into a settlement proof and executed atomically on-chain. Which capital path applies (Section~\ref{sec:capital}) determines the pipeline complexity. Three capital pipelines exist, each assembled as an atomic hook chain, with an optional Prime Trade routing step on the final transfer:

\textbf{Direct-funded.} The underwriter deploys USDC principal directly to the borrower (or to the margin agent for Prime Trade positions); the CDP holds collateral as a liquidation hedge without supplying it to an external venue, and no hedging buffers are reserved.

\textbf{Liquidity-forwarding.} The hook chain deposits collateral into the collateral manager, supplies it to the external venue, borrows USDC against it, and transfers the USDC to the borrower. For fixed-rate borrower loans, a rate buffer is escrowed to hedge the mismatch between the borrower's fixed rate and the venue's variable rate; variable-rate loans reference the committed index and size reserves according to the underwriter's policy.

\textbf{Liquidity-forwarding with collateral swap.} For collateral not directly supported by the selected external venue (e.g.\ LDO or UNI), the market may authorize a swap hook that converts the pledged collateral into a venue-listed intermediate asset (e.g.\ LDO $\to$ WETH) through an approved AMM, RFQ venue, or aggregator before the hook chain proceeds as above. This path is available only for collateral assets, intermediate assets, swap adapters, and execution routes admitted by the market's zero-knowledge programs and, where applicable, by the underwriter's committed policy.

The swap hook is executed with explicit price-protection constraints committed in the settlement leaf (minimum output, deadline, maximum slippage). If these constraints are not satisfied at execution time, the hook reverts and the origination does not settle. Both a rate buffer and a collateral conversion reserve are escrowed; the conversion reserve is sized by the risk engine to cover stressed round-trip execution costs. In canonical \daloc{} markets, assets or routes with insufficient oracle quality, shallow executable liquidity, or unavailable liquidation paths are rejected rather than routed through the swap path.

\textbf{Hyperliquid margin.} For Prime Trade positions, the hook chain follows either pipeline above but diverts at the final step: instead of transferring USDC to the borrower, a bridge hook routes it to a per-loan margin agent on HyperEVM, which deposits it as margin (Section~\ref{sec:crosschain}). Collateral never leaves Ethereum.

\subsection{Refinancing}

Not every loan runs to its original maturity. A borrower refinances by posting a single refinancing intent; the matching engine treats it identically to a new origination request. On match, the existing loan closes and a new one originates atomically within the same settlement proof. When the same underwriter is creditor on both sides of a direct-funded loan, refinancing is a pure accounting event: no capital moves, only terms change. When the creditor differs or the loan involves an external venue, repayment and new funding settle atomically. For Prime Trade positions, refinancing applies to the credit terms on Ethereum; margin capital on HyperEVM remains in place unless the position is fully closed and re-originated.

\subsection{Close and Liquidation}

Close is permitted at or after maturity (mid-term exit is available only through refinancing, as described above); either counterparty can produce the close proof independently~\cite{daloc-decent2026}. On voluntary close, the borrower deposits the principal asset into the loan's debt contract. After interest accrual is verified (Section~\ref{sec:tokens}), the creditor (underwriter or direct lender) redeems its position and a close hook chain repays the variable debt on the external venue, withdraws the collateral, and returns it to the borrower (with a reverse swap for collateral-swap loans). Hedging surplus stays with the capital provider as yield. Direct-funded loans skip the external unwind: the borrower repays, and collateral is released directly. For Prime Trade positions, the close hook chain first recalls capital from the destination venue via the bridge before repaying the source-chain debt.

If the borrower has not repaid at maturity, the creditor produces a close proof that establishes the owed amount; the contract writes an enforcement authorization flag, enabling any liquidator to execute under the same flag-gated model described below~\cite{daloc-decent2026}.

The external unwind is subject to venue-level liquidity and liveness constraints. If the venue cannot provide the required borrow liquidity at origination, or if utilization, borrow rate, pause status, or available liquidity violates the bounds committed in the quote, the origination hook reverts and the loan does not settle. For active liquidity-forwarding loans, a stressed venue can increase variable funding costs, delay collateral withdrawal, or force execution through a slower liquidation route. These risks are modeled by the risk engine through rate buffers, collateral-conversion reserves, venue exposure limits, and liquidation-latency assumptions.

If a close or liquidation cannot fully unwind the external venue position because the venue has insufficient available liquidity, withdrawals are paused, or the adapter reports a failed unwind, the loan enters a pending-unwind state. Debt repayment and recovered assets are ring-fenced, borrower withdrawals are delayed until the external position is unwound or auctioned, and any excess funding or execution cost is charged first against loan-specific reserves. Residual shortfall follows the loss waterfall (Section~\ref{sec:lp}). \daloc{} cannot remove external venue liquidity risk; it makes the risk explicit, priced, reserved, and isolated per loan.

Liquidation triggers when the health factor drops below~1:
\begin{equation}
  \HF = \frac{C_{\mathrm{val}}\,\theta}{D} < 1,
  \label{eq:healthfactor}
\end{equation}
where $C_{\mathrm{val}}$ is the collateral's current market value, $\theta$ the loan's liquidation threshold (set at origination, strictly above the origination LTV), and $D$ the outstanding debt including accrued interest. Because $D$ grows with interest accrual, the risk engine constrains $(\theta, \lambda, r, T)$ so that the health factor remains above~1 at maturity under constant collateral value. For fixed-rate positions this is a deterministic check; for variable-rate positions the constraint is probabilistic, sized so that HF remains above~1 under the stressed rate scenarios produced by the precomputation kernel (Section~\ref{subsec:provable-risk-engine}).

Liquidation uses a flag-gated, permissionless execution model.

\textbf{Detection.} A health-check sweep (produced by the operator) or a per-position proof (producible by either counterparty) computes current true debt internally from the position's rate model and elapsed time since last on-chain accrual (read via storage proof); for variable-rate positions the circuit also reads the external rate index via storage proof. The health assessment therefore does not depend on accrual proofs having been submitted recently. The circuit reads collateral price and liquidation threshold from on-chain state via storage proofs, asserting $\HF < 1$. On verification, the contract writes an \emph{authorization flag} for each breached position. Detection is identity-agnostic and capital-free: the prover reveals nothing beyond the breach. Per-position enforcement is not a fallback; it is a guaranteed, immediate capability of either counterparty, independent of operator liveness~\cite{daloc-decent2026}.

\textbf{Recovery window.} Between detection and execution, the borrower may add collateral or partially repay debt to restore $\HF > 1$. A subsequent sweep or per-position health attestation proof clears the flag if the position has genuinely recovered. The staleness bound on oracle readings prevents gaming via stale favorable prices. Between flag-setting and flag-clearing, a liquidator may execute against a position whose current health is above threshold; the borrower is compensated at execution-time oracle price (receiving residual collateral after debt is paid) but loses the bonus fraction. Timely health attestation by the borrower is the mitigation~\cite{daloc-decent2026}.

\textbf{Execution.} Once an authorization flag exists, any liquidator can execute without permission from operator, underwriter, or governance. Liquidation is atomic at the contract level: the first valid executor claims the bonus. The liquidator repays outstanding debt $R$ via the debt token contract and seizes collateral worth $R \cdot (1 + \beta)$ from the CDP, where $\beta$ is the liquidation bonus fixed at origination. The contract reads the oracle price at execution time, ensuring the price reflects conditions at the moment of liquidation. For liquidity-forwarding loans, the seizure hook chain unwinds the external venue position; if the venue cannot fully unwind (paused withdrawals, insufficient liquidity), the loan enters the pending-unwind state described above. Residual collateral beyond the liquidator's claim is returned to the borrower. Positions where collateral value falls below outstanding debt become uneconomic to liquidate; these bad-debt losses are absorbed by the loss waterfall (Section~\ref{sec:lp}).

Because enforcement is authorization-gated (the flag must exist) rather than proof-gated at execution time, the contract reads the oracle directly during the liquidation transaction. Flash loans eliminate the capital requirement for liquidators~\cite{perez2021,qin2021}: borrow the debt, seize collateral at a discount, sell, repay, keep the difference.

Because each loan is isolated, bad debt cannot cascade across positions. Shortfalls reduce underwriter NAV through impaired debt-token value (Section~\ref{sec:lp}).
