\section{Protocol Architecture}
\label{sec:architecture}

All products share a single platform layer, extensible to new products without modifying core contracts.

\subsection{Order Book}

All loan activity flows through a single order book. Borrower requests and solver quotes are structurally identical---both specify collateral, principal, rate, term, and execution path---and the matching engine treats them symmetrically. The matcher selects the best rate for the borrower among criteria-compatible quotes; the settlement proof verifies that no better match was skipped.

Participation requires no capital commitment. A borrower or solver signs an off-chain intent and grants a token allowance to the hub, authorizing a future transfer but moving nothing. Capital is transferred only when an epoch's settlement proof executes the match on-chain; until then, funds remain in the participant's wallet.

\subsection{Criteria-Based Matching}

Beyond price compatibility, a match must satisfy \emph{criteria}: typed attributes attached to an intent --- counterparty allowlists, jurisdictional restrictions, KYC-tier requirements, or sanctions-screen attestations. The matcher treats a pair as compatible only if each side's criteria are satisfied by the other's attributes. There is no separate permissioned market; entries in one order book vary only in how restrictive they are.

Criteria originate from three sources: the intent author (self-asserted), on-chain attestations or signed claims (counterparty attributes), and the market's program hashes (market-level). Criteria can be committed-only --- stored as a hash, evaluated inside the proof, revealing only the boolean outcome. The default is open: unrestricted intents match against anyone.

\subsection{Markets}

\daloc{} is organized around isolated, immutable lending markets. Each market is identified by a deterministic hash of seven parameters:
\begin{equation}
  \begin{split}
    \mathsf{marketId} &= \khash(c,\, \mathit{chainId}_p,\, p, \\
    &\qquad h_{\mathit{rate}},\, h_{\mathit{liq}},\, h_{\mathit{di}},\, h_{\mathit{cmi}})
  \end{split}
  \label{eq:marketid}
\end{equation}
where $c$ is the collateral asset, $\mathit{chainId}_p$ the principal chain, $p$ the principal asset, and $h_{\mathit{rate}}, h_{\mathit{liq}}, h_{\mathit{di}}, h_{\mathit{cmi}}$ are zero-knowledge program hashes governing interest accrual, liquidation execution, debt-issuer state transition, and collateral-manager-issuer state transition respectively. All parameters are fixed at creation: a different rate model, liquidation rule, collateral policy, or criteria requirement constitutes a different market.

Markets are created permissionlessly through the \emph{hub} contract (DalocHub). The hub is the sole token rail: all user-facing ERC-20 transfers route through it, creating a single authorization point for capital flows, intent cancellations, and global pause.

Different markets can encode different collateral strategies: a \emph{classical} market locks collateral as a liquidation hedge, while a more aggressive market can permit managed rehypothecation. The choice is a market-definition decision, not a protocol-level toggle.

Each market deploys issuer contracts on both the collateral and principal chains; each funded loan creates per-loan contracts at deterministic \texttt{CREATE2} addresses. Per-loan contracts are isolated---one per loan, not shared across borrowers---so one position's risk cannot cascade to another.

\subsection{Hook Composition}

Lending operations are expressed as atomic hook chains attached to settlement events. Each transfer in the settlement tree carries optional \emph{pre-hooks} and \emph{post-hooks}: pre-hooks execute before the transfer (e.g.\ deploy a CDP), the transfer moves collateral or principal, and post-hooks execute after (e.g.\ supply to an external venue, borrow, bridge). All steps must succeed or the entire operation reverts.

Each hook is assembled by the solver and committed into the settlement tree before on-chain execution. A hook's permitted operations are constrained by the market's ZK programs: the settlement proof verifies that every hook is authorized and that the resulting state satisfies the market's invariants. Borrowers sign high-level intents; solvers compete to attach the cheapest valid execution plan. Intent cancellation is always available (even when the protocol is paused) and takes effect at the next epoch boundary.

\subsection{Solvers}

The Harvest Vault operator is the canonical solver, participating in the order book like any other.

Independent solvers coexist on the same book. A \emph{risk-taking} solver funds from its own balance sheet; a \emph{passive} solver routes through existing money-market liquidity (Aave~\cite{aave2026}, Morpho~\cite{morphoblue2023}, Spark~\cite{spark2023}) by deploying pledged collateral on established venues. Both modes produce structurally identical entries; the best terms win. Routing and capital sourcing are solver-level concerns, making the platform extensible to new venues without core contract changes.
