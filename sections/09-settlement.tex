\section{Settlement and Verification}
\label{sec:settlement}

\subsection{Event-Driven Settlement}

Settlement is event-driven: each proven action executes atomically on submission, with no batch window and no global ordering. The detailed mechanics of proof submission, keeper economics, fee replacement, and cross-chain failure recovery are specified in the companion paper~\cite{daloc-decent2026}; this section covers the settlement framework as it relates to the protocol's product and risk architecture.

\subsection{Settlement Programs}

On-chain contracts are settlement executors: they verify proofs and execute committed transfers and hook chains but contain no market-specific logic. Market behavior (interest computation, liquidation detection, oracle selection, collateral policy, and compliance) is computed by zero-knowledge programs off-chain. Those programs output facts: hash-bound statements tying a program hash, market identifier, chain, prior state, new state, and any computed values to a verified proof.

The contracts do not recompute the market logic. Instead, they reconstruct the expected fact hash from their fixed parameters and the submitted leaf or state transition, check that the facts registry marks that fact as valid, and apply the corresponding transfer, accrual, liquidation, or update only if the fact is present. What is verified on-chain is therefore not an oracle feed or risk model directly, but the existence of a valid proof that the declared program produced the declared output from the declared inputs.

Programs split by economic cadence. \textbf{Matching} is one-time per loan: transfers, debt issuance, CDP authorization, and criteria-compatibility evaluation (Section~\ref{sec:architecture}). \textbf{Interest accrual} is per-action, submittable by any party for any debt at any time. \textbf{Health-check sweep} evaluates all active positions, flagging those that breach thresholds. \textbf{Per-position enforcement} proves a single position breaches its threshold, producible by either counterparty independently of the operator. \textbf{Close} is terminal after repayments and accrual catch up; either counterparty can produce the close proof.

All programs submit results through a single \emph{facts registry} that accepts a fact hash and returns whether it is valid. Two on-chain fact structures carry all market state. A \textbf{state-transition fact} authorizes a state update, binding the settlement program, chain, issuer, and the previous and next roots (each encoding the full set of Merkle roots). An \textbf{interest fact} authorizes accrual on one loan's debt, binding the rate program, chain, debt identifier, accrual window, and computed interest amount. The on-chain contract reconstructs the expected fact from its fixed parameters, checks the registry, and applies values only if valid. Matching, origination, liquidation-detection, close, and enforcement proofs produce state-transition facts that incorporate their respective logic; the two on-chain structures are the common execution interface through which all program types take effect.

\subsection{Commitment Registry}

Each operator maintains an on-chain commitment registry scoped per market~\cite{daloc-decent2026}. For every active position the registry stores a commitment hash alongside a storage-proved counter. The counter makes monitoring completeness enforceable: a health-check sweep proof must reference exactly as many positions as the storage-proved counter records at the observed block. Position terms (rates, thresholds, maturity dates, counterparty identities) remain off-chain as pre-images; only commitment hashes and the count appear on-chain. The companion paper analyzes the resulting privacy model and its limitations~\cite{daloc-decent2026}.

\subsection{Market and Loan Contracts}

Each market has two contracts. The \textbf{collateral manager issuer} (collateral chain) manages state, executes Merkle-authorized transfers, and deploys per-loan collateral managers. The \textbf{debt issuer} (principal chain) manages state and deploys per-loan issued debt tokens. Neither contains market-specific logic.

Each loan also has two contracts. The \textbf{collateral manager} (CDP) holds pledged assets and executes hook-composed pipelines; permitted operations are defined by the market's ZK programs. The \textbf{issued debt} is a per-loan ERC-20 tracking accrual via verified facts and partitioning repayment into principal reserve, interest reserve, and protocol fees (Section~\ref{sec:tokens}).

When position assets live on different chains, contracts deploy independently on each and storage proof finality lag applies. Cross-chain partial failures are detected and recovered via storage proofs~\cite{daloc-decent2026}.

Chain downtime is treated as a liveness failure, not a correctness failure. The affected issuer or adapter cannot execute hooks or process lifecycle events until block production resumes; the market resumes from its last valid state root. During the halt, economic risk can continue to move on other chains (oracle prices change, margin positions gain or lose value), so markets treat chain downtime as a modeled liveness risk in their admissibility checks, liquidation-latency bounds, and underwriter commitments.

\subsection{Oracle Freedom and Permissionless Markets}

Oracle logic is embedded in the market's zero-knowledge programs. Programs read price or rate data from on-chain contracts via storage proofs, enforcing freshness inside the proof rather than trusting off-chain feeds. Variable-rate positions carry a second oracle dependency: the external rate index referenced by the accrual circuit. Markets can be created from governance-registered program hashes without further per-market approval; registering wholly new program types requires hub governance. The protocol verifies program outputs but does not audit program logic. Assessing a market's risk properties is the participant's responsibility.

\subsection{System Invariants}

Five product-layer invariants hold across all states. The companion paper~\cite{daloc-decent2026} defines five safety invariants that overlap with and extend the list below, adding no unauthorized movement and no liquidation without detection; together they form the full security model:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Token conservation.} For every asset, total balances across all market contracts equal deposited collateral plus supplied principal minus withdrawals. No operation creates or destroys tokens.
  \item \textbf{No settlement without proof.} Every state transition requires a valid zero-knowledge proof registered in the facts registry. No state change can occur without cryptographic verification.
  \item \textbf{No selective omission.} Health-check sweeps must cover all active positions; the storage-proved counter rejects incomplete sets.
  \item \textbf{Position isolation.} Each loan's contracts are independently deployed, independently liquidatable, and share no mutable state with any other loan. One position's insolvency cannot reach into another's contract state. (Economic correlation through a shared underwriter may affect NAV but does not violate contract-level isolation.)
  \item \textbf{Exit without cooperation.} Either counterparty can produce per-position close, enforcement, and accrual proofs independently. When a position is healthy and not yet matured but the operator has been absent past the escape timeout, any pre-image holder can settle at oracle price.
\end{enumerate}
