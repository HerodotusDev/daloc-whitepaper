\section{Cross-Chain Exposure}
\label{sec:crosschain}

The protocol treats cross-chain operation as the default case---a same-chain loan is the degenerate instance where collateral and principal chains happen to coincide. Cross-chain loans involve two kinds of operations at different layers.

\textbf{Authorization} is a protocol-level concern: proving that the borrower actually pledged collateral on the collateral chain so the principal chain's contracts can act on it. \daloc{} handles authorization with ZK storage proofs via the Herodotus Data Processor (HDP). HDP produces verifiable execution traces that combine storage proofs over historical state with MMR-backed chain history verification across EVM chains and Starknet. A single proof can read the borrower's collateral balance on the collateral chain, the CDP's position on the external lending venue, and the venue's current utilization---composing cross-chain and cross-protocol state reads into one verifiable claim.

\textbf{Token movement} is a solver-level concern: physically moving the borrowed asset to its destination chain. The protocol does not move funds---solvers do, as the execution side of fulfilling a borrower's request. Solvers choose the path; for USDC, Circle's Cross-Chain Transfer Protocol (CCTP) is the common choice. Bridge hooks compose token movement into the same atomic hook chain as the rest of the origination pipeline. The two layers are independent.

\subsection{Deterministic Addressing}

The hub contract deploys at the same address on every supported chain, so markets, allowances, and registry lookups align. Per-loan CDPs and debt tokens are deployed at deterministic addresses derived from the hub and the loan identifier---the platform computes addresses at quote time, hours before on-chain deployment. Matching and risk run off-chain; each chain advances epochs with its own five Merkle roots and epoch sequence, verified locally via Merkle inclusion proofs.

A loan can lock collateral on chain~A while the debt token and lender claims live on chain~B---coordinated via shared epoch commitments and cross-chain address derivation. New destinations require only new bridge adapters and receiver contracts whitelisted on the hub; the origination pattern is not venue-specific.

\subsection{Hyperliquid Pipeline}

When a borrower accepts a margin-path quote, the Ethereum leg settles atomically through a hook chain: epoch roots are posted, the per-loan CDP is deployed, and hooks execute---deposit collateral, source USDC (either directly from the vault or by supplying collateral to an external venue and borrowing), and bridge. Instead of transferring USDC to the borrower, a bridge hook burns the USDC via CCTP, with the destination set to a per-loan \emph{margin agent} on HyperEVM whose address was computed at quote time via \texttt{CREATE2}.

CCTP attesters and relayers mint USDC to the margin agent on HyperEVM. The margin agent deposits the USDC into HyperCore as margin in its own per-position account---the capital never enters the borrower's personal wallet. The borrower selects perpetual positions through the platform's interface; the margin agent constrains executable actions to perpetual trading only, blocking withdrawals, spot transfers, and external lending.

Close or liquidation on a margin loan first recalls capital from Hyperliquid, then unwinds the Ethereum leg. The margin agent transfers funds from the perp account to spot, withdraws to HyperEVM, burns via CCTP back to a receiver contract on Ethereum that forwards USDC to the CDP. Only then does the standard close hook chain run---repay the external venue, release collateral, settle buffers. Collateral never crosses to HyperEVM; only USDC round-trips while collateral stays on Ethereum.

\subsection{Under-Collateralized Leverage}

From the lender's perspective, every current loan is fully collateralized on Ethereum: pledged assets in the external venue back the variable debt and the vault's claim. From the borrower's perspective, the position is under-collateralized relative to venue exposure.

Consider a borrower who deposits 40~ETH (worth \$80{,}000 at \$2{,}000/ETH). The CDP supplies the ETH to the external venue and borrows \$50{,}000 USDC at a 62.5\% borrow ratio. The \$50{,}000 is bridged to a protocol-controlled margin agent on Hyperliquid, where it serves as margin for perpetual positions at up to 5$\times$ leverage---controlling \$250{,}000 of notional exposure. The borrower's \$80{,}000 of collateral backs \$250{,}000 of venue exposure: a 3.1$\times$ effective leverage that would be impossible without selling the underlying ETH.
